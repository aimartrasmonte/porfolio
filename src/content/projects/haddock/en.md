In spring 2024, together with three classmates from ETSEIB, we built a 1 m radio-controlled sailboat for [RC Sailing Barcelona](http://rcsailingbarcelona.com/), a regatta between engineering schools in Catalonia, with **90 days, €500** and strict rules limiting size, weight and watertightness. I designed and built the **hull**, which ended up becoming my bachelor's thesis.

## Design concept

A boat this small hits a speed limit of just 2.4 knots when sailing "in" the water. To get past it, the hull has to **plane** on top of it, like a surfboard. To make planing easier, Haddock has a wide, flat-bottomed V hull that provides plenty of lifting surface when the wind heels the boat, with a narrow, low-volume bow that cuts through the waves.

## CAD

The design was done with:

- **MaxSurf** for the hull shape and the stability and resistance calculations.
- **SolidWorks** for the final model: a hollow hull with 1.5 mm walls, split into 4 parts for printing, with an internal frame structure to spread the loads from the mast, fin and rudder.
- **Bambu Studio and Cura** for slicing the 3D prints.

[[3d]]

## Manufacturing

Manufacturing revolves around one idea: **the 3D-printed shell acts as both mould and structure**. I laminated the fibreglass straight on top of it, with no need to make a mould, giving the hull the strength it needed and leaving the plastic inside as structure. I made the 2 kg ballast by melting down old diving weights in a sand mould.

## Results

- **3rd of 12 teams** and **2nd best speed time** (50 m in 50 s).
- Passed technical inspection on the first attempt.
- **€509** total cost, 1.8% over budget.

[[13-18]]

**What I'd change:** the boat ended up 25% heavier than planned, and the carbon rudder shaft broke just before the final with no time for a makeshift repair, leaving us last in the final race: 3rd place overall, despite being the favourites.
